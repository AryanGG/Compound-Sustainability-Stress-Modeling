03 / INDICATORS

Which Stressors Are Driving the SSI?

The Compound SSI is built from five independent stress indicators, each normalised to a 0–1 scale. This chart decomposes the SSI into its components so you can see which specific stressors are rising, seasonal, or persistently elevated.

🔬
Indicator definitions: Heat Stress — derived from temperature, dewpoint (Heat Index) and land surface temperature anomalies. Water Stress — precipitation deficit below climatological normal + soil moisture depletion. Pollution — PM₂.₅ concentration relative to WHO annual guideline (5 µg/m³). Vegetation Stress — NDVI anomaly below seasonal baseline (inverted: less green = more stress). Urban Vulnerability — composite of BPL%, slum%, elderly population%, and (inverse) literacy rate.

04 / CLIMATOLOGY

What Seasonal and Structural Patterns Exist?

These charts reveal the underlying climatological signal in the SSI — recurring seasonal peaks, inter-annual trends, and the statistical relationships between stress indicators. Understanding these patterns is essential for anticipatory planning.

🔄
Seasonal patterns: In Indian cities, stress typically peaks in pre-monsoon months (April–June) due to heat, and again in post-monsoon months in northern cities due to air pollution (stubble burning + winter inversions). The heatmap makes it easy to spot these recurring cycles year-over-year. Correlations: Strongly correlated indicators (dark red in the matrix) tend to co-spike — interventions that address one may help reduce the other.

Values near +1 (dark red) mean the two indicators tend to spike together — a compound risk signal. Values near 0 (white) indicate independent stressors. Values near −1 (dark blue) are rare and suggest opposite seasonal patterns.

About This Report

The Compound Sustainability Stress Index (SSI) is a composite, spatio-temporal measure of environmental stress computed for Indian cities using multi-source geospatial data. It combines five independently normalised stress indicators — heat, water, pollution, vegetation, and urban vulnerability — into a single 0–1 score using PCA-derived weights adjusted by city-specific risk multipliers. The H3 hexagonal grid at resolution 8 (~0.74 km² per cell) provides a uniform spatial framework for all computations. All indicators use a rolling 3-year baseline for temporal normalisation.

Data Sources
  • ERA5 Reanalysis — Copernicus CDS (ECMWF)
  • Sentinel-2 L2A NDVI — Microsoft Planetary Computer
  • MODIS MOD11A2 LST — NASA / Planetary Computer
  • PM₂.₅ Annual Mean — NASA SEDAC SDEI
  • Road & buildings — OpenStreetMap via OSMnx
  • Vulnerability priors — Census of India 2011
SSI Methodology
  • H3 resolution 8 spatial grid
  • Monthly temporal aggregation
  • Z-score normalisation (3-yr rolling baseline)
  • PCA-based composite weighting
  • City-specific weight adjustments
  • k-means archetype clustering (k=6)
  • 90th-percentile anomaly detection
