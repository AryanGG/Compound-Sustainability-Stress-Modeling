04 / CLIMATOLOGY

What Seasonal and Structural Patterns Exist?

These charts reveal the underlying climatological signal in the SSI — recurring seasonal peaks, inter-annual trends, and the statistical relationships between stress indicators. Understanding these patterns is essential for anticipatory planning.

🔄
Seasonal patterns: In Indian cities, stress typically peaks in pre-monsoon months (April–June) due to heat, and again in post-monsoon months in northern cities due to air pollution (stubble burning + winter inversions). The heatmap makes it easy to spot these recurring cycles year-over-year. Correlations: Strongly correlated indicators (dark red in the matrix) tend to co-spike — interventions that address one may help reduce the other.

08 / LIMITATIONS

Methodological Boundaries & Disclosures

A rigorous, defensible science framework requires upfront disclosure of data constraints and model boundaries. Full technical justifications and sensitivity results are detailed in docs/limitations.md.

⚠️

Explicit Model Boundaries & Constraints

  • Coarse Resolution of Pollution: CAMS EAC4 surface PM2.5 reanalysis is ~0.75° (~80 km) grid resolution. It provides a monthly city-wide temporal signal, not intra-urban spatial discrimination across individual H3 cells.
  • Spatial Uniformity of Water Stress: ERA5 total precipitation and volumetric soil moisture drive water stress at ~11 km resolution; between-month variance accounts for ~99% of total variance, reflecting broad climatic seasonality rather than hyper-local drainage variations.
  • Static Exposure Indicators: Socio-economic vulnerability (BPL, slum household share) and built infrastructure metrics are static priors derived from Census and baseline layers.
  • Missing Observation Window: November 2022 raw data has a satellite collection gap documented in the data dictionary.
  • Modest Spatial Agreement with Independent LST: Heat stress exhibits modest within-month spatial correlation with held-out MODIS LST (mean within-month ρ ≈ 0.17), indicating spatial micro-climate complexity beyond single-sensor proxies.
  • Modeling Choices (γ and q): Compound boost parameters (γ = 0.30, 90th percentile q) are explicit modeling choices; robustness grid sweeps demonstrate that spatial rankings remain stable while levels and compound shares adjust smoothly.
  • Absence of Outcome Validation: SSI represents an environmental hazard exposure composite; it is not calibrated against empirical morbidity, mortality, or financial loss records.
  • Quantile-Based Relative Bands: Risk bands (Low, Moderate, High, Extreme) are defined by pooled empirical quartiles across reference cities, rather than absolute physiological damage thresholds.
  • Cross-City Sample Size: Two reference cities (Mumbai, Coimbatore) allow cross-city comparative analysis under frozen baselines, but do not support city-level rank correlation statistics.

About This Report (v2 Canonical)

The Compound Sustainability Stress Index (SSI v2) is a composite, spatio-temporal measure of environmental hazard exposure across Indian cities. It combines five independently normalised physical stress drivers — heat, water, pollution, vegetation, and exposure — into a single composite score using a primary equal weighting scheme calibrated on a frozen pooled baseline. An interactive compound boost term couples co-occurring extreme heat and water stress.

v2 Methodology & Reproducibility
  • H3 resolution 8 spatial framework (~0.74 km²)
  • Monthly temporal aggregation (2020–2022)
  • Pooled frozen reference baseline (2nd–98th percentile scaling)
  • Primary equal weighting across 5 indicators
  • Smooth coupling compound boost (γ = 0.30, q = 0.90)
  • Stationary hex-level archetypes (KMeans k=2)
